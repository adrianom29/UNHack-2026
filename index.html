How the forecast works

For each item, SmartPrep estimates tomorrow's demand from your saved days. Recent days count more than older ones, and each day of the week gets its own level (a busy Friday is not averaged in with a quiet Monday). On days you sold out, demand was at least what you sold, so SmartPrep estimates how much more you could have sold instead of assuming you sold everything people wanted. It then uses the price and cost on your menu to weigh the cost of leftovers against the cost of running out.

With fewer than 7 days of records an item's plan is marked provisional. Once items have more than 7 days, SmartPrep checks itself: it replays your last 4 weeks, planning each day only from the days before it, and compares that with what you actually made and sold. The waste and profit figures come from that check. Until then they are marked as estimates. Past profit uses today's menu prices and costs, so a price change does not count as an improvement.

These are estimates, not guarantees. On sold-out days the check has to estimate how much more would have sold, which makes the waste figure slightly optimistic. Days with nothing made are left out. Profit here means sales minus the cost of what you made; rent, wages and other fixed costs are not included.
